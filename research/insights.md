# Problem statement
Students struggle to track spending and save consistently. They overspend, lack visibility, and don’t use budgeting tools.

# Why PayNest exists
PayNest helps students track allowances, manage side income, and save easily with simple, student‑friendly tools.

# Assumptions
- Students rely on allowances or irregular income.
- Overspending happens on food, transport, and social events.
- Few use budgeting tools; most track mentally.
- Gamification and peer influence can drive saving.
- Trust grows if the app feels student‑focused.

# Student struggles
- Tracking money from different sources.
- Saving small amounts regularly.
- Simple budgeting for student life.
- Peer pressure to spend.
- Trust and transparency.

# Research questions
- How do students manage allowances and side income?
- What triggers overspending?
- What motivates saving?
- What trust signals matter?
- Why do current apps fail?

# Next steps
Plan 5–10 student interviews and a short survey to validate assumptions.


## Day 5 – UX Wireframes + Usability Assumptions

### Wireframes
- Login: email, password, login button.
- Dashboard: balance summary, quick buttons, navigation bar.
- Transactions: list view, filter categories.
- Savings Goal: goal name, target amount, frequency, progress bar.

### Usability Evidence
#### Tasks users should complete
- Log in and reach dashboard.
- Record or view a transaction.
- Create a savings goal.

#### Potential confusion points
- Login: email vs student ID.
- Dashboard: too many buttons may overwhelm.
- Transactions: unclear category filters.
- Savings goal: frequency options may confuse.
